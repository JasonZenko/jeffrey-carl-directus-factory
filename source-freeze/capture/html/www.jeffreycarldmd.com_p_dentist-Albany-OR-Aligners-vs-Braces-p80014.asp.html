Aligners vs Braces

Albany, OR


A straight, healthy smile can improve both oral health and confidence. Teeth that are properly aligned are easier to clean, distribute biting pressure more evenly, and support comfortable jaw function. Orthodontic treatment focuses on gradually guiding teeth into improved positions using controlled pressure over time.

Modern dentistry offers more than one way to accomplish this goal. Traditional braces remain a reliable method for correcting alignment concerns, while clear aligners provide an alternative that many patients find appealing for everyday life. Each system works toward the same objective, but they do so in different ways and with different advantages.

How Orthodontic Treatment Works


Orthodontic treatment is based on the principle that gentle, consistent pressure can gradually move teeth within the jawbone. The bone surrounding each tooth responds to this pressure by breaking down and rebuilding in small increments. As this process occurs, the tooth slowly shifts into its new position.

Traditional braces use a series of brackets attached to the front of the teeth and connected by an archwire. The wire is adjusted periodically to apply pressure that guides teeth into alignment. Small elastic bands or additional appliances may also be used to address specific alignment concerns.

Clear aligners use a series of custom-made trays that fit snugly over the teeth. Each aligner in the sequence is slightly different from the one before it. As patients move through the series, each tray places gentle pressure on certain teeth, encouraging gradual movement. The aligners are worn for most of the day and replaced with the next set according to the treatment schedule.

Both approaches rely on careful planning and monitoring. Treatment plans are designed using detailed images and measurements to determine how the teeth should move throughout the process.

Aligners vs Braces


Clear aligners and traditional braces are both effective tools for orthodontic treatment. They share the same goal of correcting alignment concerns such as crowding, spacing, and bite issues. However, their design, daily wear, and maintenance differ in several important ways.

Choosing between them often depends on the complexity of the orthodontic concern, lifestyle preferences, and how closely patients can follow wear instructions.

Visibility


One of the most noticeable differences between aligners and braces is their appearance.

Braces are attached to the front of the teeth and are visible when speaking or smiling. Modern brackets are smaller and more streamlined than older versions, but they remain noticeable.

Clear aligners are made from transparent material that fits closely over the teeth. Because of this design, they are much less noticeable during everyday activities. Many patients appreciate the subtle appearance, especially in professional or social settings.

Comfort


Orthodontic treatment often involves periods of adjustment as teeth begin to move.

Braces include brackets and wires that may occasionally irritate the cheeks or lips. Orthodontic wax can be used to reduce friction during these times.

Aligners are made from smooth plastic, which eliminates the presence of brackets and wires. Many patients find the smooth surface more comfortable against the soft tissues of the mouth. However, the gentle pressure that moves the teeth can still create temporary soreness when switching to a new aligner.

Maintenance


The two treatment options require different types of care and attention.

Braces are fixed to the teeth and remain in place throughout treatment. Regular visits allow the dentist to adjust the archwire and monitor progress. Patients do not need to remove anything on a daily basis, but they do need to be careful about protecting the brackets and wires.

Aligners are removable, which means patients take them out for meals and cleaning. This flexibility is convenient, but it also requires discipline. For aligners to work properly, they must be worn for most of the day:
•  Aligners are typically worn for about 20 to 22 hours each day
•  They are removed for eating and drinking anything other than water
•  Patients must remember to place them back in after meals

Consistency is important with aligners because extended time without wearing them can slow progress.

Oral Hygiene


Maintaining oral hygiene during orthodontic treatment is essential for protecting teeth and gums.

Braces require extra care while brushing and flossing because food particles can become trapped around brackets and wires. Special tools such as floss threaders or interdental brushes can help clean between the teeth and under the wire.

Aligners simplify oral hygiene because they are removable. Patients can brush and floss normally after taking out the trays. The aligners themselves should also be cleaned regularly to prevent buildup and maintain clarity.

Modifications to Eating


Eating habits can change during orthodontic treatment, particularly with traditional braces.

Certain foods can damage brackets or wires, which means patients are usually advised to avoid items that are very hard, sticky, or chewy. Examples may include hard candies, popcorn kernels, and sticky caramels.

Aligners allow more flexibility with meals because they are removed before eating. Patients can generally continue enjoying their usual foods, provided they brush their teeth before placing the aligners back in the mouth.

Treatment Time


The length of orthodontic treatment varies depending on the complexity of the case and how the teeth respond to movement.

Braces have been used for decades to treat a wide range of orthodontic concerns, including more complex bite issues. In some cases, they may provide greater control over tooth movement.

Aligners are effective for many alignment concerns and continue to expand in their capabilities. Treatment timelines can be similar to braces in some situations, but success with aligners depends heavily on wearing them as instructed.

During treatment, regular checkups allow the dentist to evaluate progress and make any necessary adjustments to the plan.

Choosing The Right Orthodontic Option


Both braces and clear aligners are designed to create healthier, more balanced smiles. The most appropriate option depends on the patient's orthodontic needs, lifestyle preferences, and the treatment goals established during the consultation.

Our dentists, Dr. Carl and Dr. Brunson, will evaluate several factors when recommending a treatment approach.
•  The position and spacing of the teeth
•  The relationship between the upper and lower jaw
•  The overall health of the teeth and gums
•  Daily habits that may affect treatment consistency

For some patients, traditional braces may provide the most predictable path to correcting complex alignment concerns. For others, clear aligners offer an appealing option that fits comfortably into daily routines.
