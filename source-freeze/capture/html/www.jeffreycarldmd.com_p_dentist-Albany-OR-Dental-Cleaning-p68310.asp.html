Importance of Dental Cleanings


Dental cleanings are fundamental to maintaining optimal oral health. Despite diligent brushing and flossing at home, plaque and tartar can still accumulate over time with regular oral hygiene practices, especially in areas that are difficult to reach. Professional dental cleanings performed by dental hygienists remove plaque, tartar, and stains from the teeth, as a result, preventing the development of cavities, gum disease, and other oral health issues.

Regular dental cleanings also contribute to fresher breath and a brighter smile. They help eliminate bacteria that cause bad breath and remove surface stains, resulting in a noticeably whiter appearance of the teeth. Moreover, dental cleanings offer an opportunity for early detection of dental problems, allowing for timely intervention and treatment.

Recommended Frequency and Timing of Dental Cleanings


The frequency of dental cleanings varies depending on individual oral health needs. For most people, dental professionals recommend scheduling a cleaning every six months. However, individuals with certain risk factors, such as a history of gum disease or a high propensity for plaque buildup, may require more frequent cleanings. Individuals being treated for gum disease may come in every three to four months.

Maintaining a consistent schedule for dental cleanings is essential for preventing the accumulation of plaque and tartar, which can lead to more serious oral health issues if left untreated. Your dentist or dental hygienist can assess your oral health status and recommend an appropriate cleaning schedule tailored to your specific needs.

Signs You Need a Dental Cleaning


There are a few key indicators which may dictate the need for a dental cleaning and exam. These can include:

Persistent Bad Breath


Persistent bad breath, also known as halitosis, can indicate the presence of bacteria in the mouth. Despite regular brushing and flossing, bacteria can accumulate on the tongue, gums, and teeth, leading to foul-smelling breath. A professional dental cleaning can effectively remove the bacteria responsible for bad breath, restoring oral freshness.

Gum Sensitivity or Bleeding


Gum sensitivity or bleeding during brushing or flossing is often a sign of gum inflammation or gingivitis. This condition occurs due to the buildup of plaque along the gumline, irritating the surrounding tissues. A dental cleaning helps remove plaque and tartar, reducing inflammation and promoting healthier gums. Contrary to popular belief, it is not normal for your gums to bleed when you brush and floss your teeth. This is not normal and indicates something is going on.

Visible Plaque or Tartar Build-Up


Visible plaque or tartar buildup on the surfaces of the teeth indicates the need for a professional dental cleaning and exam. While regular brushing and flossing at home can remove some plaque, stubborn deposits can accumulate over time and harden into tartar. This stubborn tartar can only be removed by a dental professional using specialized tools.

Dental Cleaning Techniques and Tools


During a dental cleaning, one of our dental hygienists will employ various techniques and tools to thoroughly clean your teeth and gums. These may include:

•  Scaling: Using a scaler, the hygienist removes plaque and tartar from the tooth surfaces, including hard-to-reach areas.
•  Polishing: Polishing removes surface stains and smooths the tooth surfaces, making them less susceptible to plaque accumulation.
•  Fluoride Treatment: Fluoride application helps strengthen tooth enamel and prevent cavities.
•  Oral Hygiene Education: We will provide guidance on proper brushing and flossing techniques to maintain oral health between your cleanings and exams with us.

FAQs about Dental Cleanings


Here are a few of the most commonly asked questions we receive from our patients in regard to dental cleanings.

Are dental cleanings painful?


Dental cleanings are typically not painful. Some individuals may experience some mild discomfort or sensitivity during certain procedures, such as scaling or probing, but the discomfort is usually minimal and only temporary. Our dental professionals strive to ensure patient comfort throughout the entire cleaning process.

What if I have sensitive teeth or gums?


If you have sensitive teeth or gums, be sure to inform your dental hygienist before your dental cleaning begins. We can adjust the cleaning techniques we employ and use specialized tools to help minimize discomfort. Additionally, desensitizing agents or fluoride treatments may be applied to alleviate sensitivity and strengthen tooth enamel throughout the process.

Is dental cleaning covered by insurance?


Many dental insurance plans cover routine dental cleanings and exams as part of preventive care. However, coverage may vary depending on the specific insurance plan and provider you have. It's advisable to check with your insurance company to determine your coverage for dental cleanings and any associated costs before you come in, so you will be aware of any out of pocket costs.
